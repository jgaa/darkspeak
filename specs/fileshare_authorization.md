# DarkSpeak FileShare Authorization and Exchange

## 1. Scope and ownership

Implement identity-scoped FileShare authorization and exchange policy over the effective per-contact VFS defined by `vfs.md`. One policy evaluator supplies actual VFS/server decisions, administrative "View as Contact", and structured explanations. Storage registry assignments, backend exclusions, filesystem capability/confinement, and system resource protections are absolute prerequisites, not permissions. The authorization component never handles physical paths, hashes file contents, or implements file transfers. Use async-safe, bounded, versioned persistent configuration and atomic policy generations. Groups are identity-scoped collections maintained by the general contacts/groups component; this module owns their FileShare policy rules, not membership.

Authorization, transfer admission, file acceptance, credit approval, and accounting are separate decisions. A grant to browse/download never implies enough credit or capacity to transfer. Every remote request retains (local identity, authenticated contact, effective mount, source namespace/object, operation, virtual path, configuration/authorization/assignment generations); no bare backend ID or cached decision confers access. A user's onion address is not their identity.

## 2. Permission rules and evaluation

At identity, group, and contact tiers, persist rules on virtual paths/mounts with independently tri-state permissions `UNSPECIFIED | ALLOW | DENY` for at least LIST, READ/DOWNLOAD, UPLOAD/CREATE, WRITE/REPLACE, RENAME, DELETE, RECURSE and catalog export (extend through versioned operation definitions). A separate path-level `DENIED` hides/blocks a subtree. Identity defaults ultimately resolve every permission. Rules can modify permissions without defining a mount. The VFS composes mounts; this engine evaluates their effective access and retains mount provenance.

For **each permission**: resolve the most specific explicit applicable contact rule; otherwise resolve each applicable group independently by path specificity, then merge group results (`DENY` > `ALLOW` > `UNSPECIFIED`); otherwise take the most specific explicit identity rule or definitive identity default. `UNSPECIFIED` inherits. Higher tier wins over lower tier regardless of depth. Path `DENIED` is a separate subtree barrier, overridden only by an applicable explicit grant at a higher tier for the requested path; a reopened descendant has synthetic navigation-only ancestors, not access to their denied source contents. Evaluate relevant ancestor barriers as well as operation-specific decisions. A denial of READ need not deny LIST.

`RECURSE` permits implicit descent into ordinary source subdirectories within the selected mount; `RECURSE=DENY` does not hide direct files or independently authorized explicit child mounts. Virtual navigation to an explicitly mounted descendant is distinct from source recursion. Explicit path `DENIED` blocks child mounts until a higher-tier rule reopens the relevant virtual route. Parent mount replacement shadows inherited lower-tier descendants; permission-only rules do not change topology. Conflicting incompatible group source mounts at the same path remain inaccessible with a coalesced local administrator notification. Never infer permissions from a differently mounted occurrence of the same backend object.

Define and regression-test exact precedence for inherited path rules, multi-group conflicts, reopened denied ancestors, synthetic mount containers, aliases, nested mounts and assignment boundaries. A resolved authorization result reports `Allowed | Denied | Hidden | TemporarilyUnavailable` with a safe machine-readable reason, policy generation and complete evaluated provenance; remote replies collapse hidden/denied existence as required by VFS privacy rules.

## 3. Policy resolution for exchange and quotas

All exchange and resource-policy fields are independently optional (`UNSPECIFIED` inherits), evaluated contact → merged groups → identity defaults, using the most specific explicit rule per field within each tier. Administrative hard ceilings are separately enforced and never overridden. Group merging is fixed per property, not configurable:

| Policy field | Merge among groups |
| --- | --- |
| Byte or file-count download earning ratio | Most favorable comparable ratio |
| Free/initial credit, earned-credit cap, voluntary download allowance | Highest benefit/allowance |
| Credit approval: automatic, manual, disabled | `Disabled` > `Manual` > `Automatic` |
| Upload file acceptance: automatic or manual | Manual wins |
| Duplicate-upload rejection and hash exclusions | Most restrictive; exclusion union within configured scope |
| Contact trust upload allowance / quota | Largest allowance, within hard identity/backend bounds |
| Physical/staging/temporary/cache budgets, bandwidth and transfer concurrency safety ceilings | Smallest finite limit (all hard caps remain applicable) |
| Optional audit/history policy | Most privacy-preserving |

A missing value inherits, while explicit `Unlimited` and `Disabled` are distinct policy values. Avoid interpreting zero as unspecified. A ratio can be disabled for unrestricted downloading or earning, as separately configured; downloading without credit and earning without requiring credits are independent. Byte ratios and file-count ratios are incomparable: conflicting explicit group modes produce a policy conflict requiring an explicit contact choice or administrator correction; do not silently convert units. Represent ratios as checked rational integers, never floating point. Independent favorable group fields may combine into a policy more generous than any one group; expose the complete effective combination and provenance in the UI.

The configured policy applies to future operations, not retroactively to previously accepted uploads or completed downloads. Capture an immutable effective earning policy/units and potential award on accepting each upload; later approval applies that captured award. Quotas may be revised prospectively, without silently deleting already stored content or altering past ledger entries. Explicit identity/system safety ceilings apply even if the contact policy says unlimited.

## 4. Admission, acceptance and integrity

Before accepting a remote upload, check authenticated contact, effective mount/path permissions and RECURSE/path barriers, identity storage assignment, backend `Write` capability, absolute system/backend/identity limits, contact/group quotas, staging reservations, concurrency/rate limits, and object limits. Backend reserves actual capacity before creating or extending content; never rely on a free-space check without reservations. Unknown sizes acquire bounded incremental reservations. Disk-full and quota failures are recoverable, typed outcomes. Remote failures disclose no private paths, disk geometry, excluded hashes or cross-identity presence.

A completed file is **accepted** only after entire expected size and locally calculated SHA-256 match, relevant policy/hash checks pass, and the destination backend commits through its existing durable FileManager lifecycle. Duplicate detection may reject announced hashes early, but never trusts the sender hash as proof. Scope duplicate checks explicitly (target share/identity-visible VFS, or owner-defined list); do not expose the global cross-identity index as an existence oracle. Explicit SHA-256 unwanted/exclusion lists override otherwise permissible uploads. Rejected, incomplete or duplicate uploads earn no credit.

File acceptance and credit approval are independent. A policy may permit automatic file acceptance but require operator credit approval, or require operator file acceptance before publication. Manual acceptance uses an isolated, quota-accounted quarantine/pending state inaccessible to normal peers until approved; retain validated hashes and bounded retention. File rejection releases ordinary storage/trust allowances after durable cleanup while retaining minimal anti-duplicate/accounting identifiers. Approval policies must not allow an unreviewed file to become visible where manual file acceptance is required. Approved uploads may remain stored even when their credits are revoked. Credit is never awarded before file acceptance.

## 5. Credit accounts and ledger

Maintain **separate per-local-identity, per-contact** byte and file-count credit accounts, with an append-only, durable, transactionally applied accounting ledger. Ledger records carry unique operation IDs, account, units, signed amount, type (`UploadAward`, `UploadRevocation`, `DownloadDebit`, `ManualGrant`, `ManualDeduction`, `BalanceAdjustment`), coarse timestamp and optional operator reason. Upload-related records reference immutable accepted `UploadId`; manual changes require no file ID. A current-balance materialization may be used for speed but is updated atomically with ledger state. All arithmetic is overflow-checked; preserve ledger history needed for idempotency and reconciliation.

Each accepted upload has one authoritative credit-entitlement record: original uploader, identity, accepted content size/count, immutable captured award and approval state (`Pending | Approved | Revoked | Disabled`), plus a monotonic revision. State transitions are serialized, idempotent and reconcile to **one current contribution**: approving twice cannot credit twice; revoking twice cannot debit twice; reapproving adds it exactly once. Enforce this through unique transaction/transition IDs and transactional state checks, not UI behavior. Concurrent operator clicks, request retries, crashes and replays must be safe. Deleting an accepted file does not implicitly revoke its credit; toggling an entitlement never mutates unrelated manual adjustments.

Allow operator grants, deductions and atomic `Set balance` (including reset to zero from negative or positive) using ledger adjustments calculated from the locked authoritative current balance. Repeating a zero reset when already zero is a no-op. Negative balances are legal after credit revocation/deductions; they block additional credit-restricted downloads until replenished, but do not rewrite completed transfers. Optional reason fields and bounded history views support review. Pending approval is displayed separately and never spendable.

For downloads, authorize path access first, then evaluate required credit independently. Reserve sufficient credit before transfer admission and account for the chosen mode correctly across partial/resumed transfers, duplicate chunks, retries, cancellations and failures; settled debits must correspond to uniquely chargeable transferred content under a precisely versioned accounting rule. File-count debits must occur at most once per successfully completed file identity/transfer charge, not per retry; byte debits must not double-charge retransmitted ranges. A temporary reservation is not a final debit. Capture the applicable download policy at admission and retain a durable transfer/charge ID. Insufficient balance fails admission, except for explicitly unrestricted download policies.

## 6. Resource budgets and physical-space protection

Resource controls have two orthogonal dimensions: **scope** and **usage class**.

Scopes: shared physical storage pool/filesystem, backend, namespace, identity, contact, and active session/request. Classes: (1) permanent accepted content, (2) incomplete incoming transfer/staging, (3) operation-temporary artifacts such as complete VFS catalog exports and generated directory listings, (4) disposable reusable caches such as thumbnails and immutable catalog exports. All classes count toward common physical capacity while retaining independent class budgets for bytes, objects, outstanding reservations, concurrent writers/generators, leases and retention. Temporary artifacts and caches are private storage objects, never normal VFS files merely because they exist.

System/backend guards include configurable minimum **available bytes**, minimum **available percent** and maximum backend/pool-used bytes (e.g. stop new admissions below 10% free or above 123 GiB backend usage), with optional more restrictive critical low-space handling for active uploads/generation. Both percent and byte minimums must hold. Check pool capacity including all active reservations, retained old generations, recovery needs and other DarkSpeak namespaces sharing the physical pool. FileManager/backend enforces durable capacity reservations, quota accounting, lifecycle, cleanup and platform space checks; the host coordinates shared-pool hard ceilings where multiple backends share a filesystem. These are absolute, not mergeable user privileges. Re-evaluate admission and growth, pause/reject safely on threshold changes; cleanup/recovery must retain headroom. Another local process may fill the disk, so handle actual `DiskFull` independently.

Use independent, bounded temporary-artifact creation and read leases via an **optional common `IFileManager` capability** with default `UnsupportedCapability`, not implementation casts in normal VFS/server code. Finalize and hash a catalog artifact before transmission; bind a lease to the authorized VFS request, revoke future reads on permission withdrawal, and bound resume retention. Cache items are evictable when unleased; promote finalized temporary objects to immutable cache objects without payload duplication where supported, but that optimization is optional. Per-contact and per-identity temporary admission caps protect against catalog-export spam; equivalent work sharing is optional later. Exhausting temporary/cache budgets must not prevent routine permanent-file reads.

Application policy decides **who may request** temporary work and admission budgets. FileManager enforces **what is actually reserved, stored and reclaimed**. The VFS produces authorized catalog data but does not implement temporary physical storage or quotas. Expose safe generic resource-unavailable reasons remotely; keep actual capacity figures and internal paths local.

## 7. Decisions, revocation and inspection UI

One effective-policy resolver produces decision and full structured explanation for actual requests and the administrator's "View as Contact" inspection. Preserve every evaluated contributing rule (including overridden group ALLOW/DENY and inherited decisions) with tier, path, group/contact/rule stable IDs, provenance, effective value and whether it was decisive. The UI colors individual permission contributions by tier and uses each group's own icon/color, including losing contributors; effective result remains obvious with accessible text/icons, not color alone. Present effective quotas, earning ratio/mode, credit approval, available/pending/negative balances, limiting safety ceilings and the reason for unavailable operations.

Configuration, group membership, storage-assignment, policy and mount changes advance generations, invalidate derived views, authorization-bound cursors/subscriptions and cached permissions, and revoke affected readers, prepared/snapshot leases, writers and pending commits at safe boundaries. Recheck during subsequent reads and before irreversible commit. Authorization withdrawal is never delayed merely because the storage backend already prepared a file. Restrict operator administration, manual credit actions and detailed ledger access to privileged local context. Publish changes and decision provenance without leaking denied names, hidden counts, physical paths or identity-private data to remote contacts.

## 8. Implementation and tests

Implement in separable phases: (A) permissions/provenance with identity-scoped groups and VFS mount contexts; (B) upload acceptance/quotas and hard resource budgets; (C) ledger/credits and resumable accounting; (D) administration/credit review UI. Remote read-only FileShare can ship after A; upload/exchange requires B and C. No duplicate transfer engine or filesystem backend.

Test exhaustive permission matrices across tiers, path specificity, `UNSPECIFIED`, DENIED reopening, RECURSE and explicit child mounts, cross-mount aliasing, multiple groups and provenance icons; policy merges including incomparable ratio modes and restrictive manual approval; stale generations and revocation during read/commit; automated/manual acceptance and integrity/duplicate rejection; concurrency/replay/double-click approval idempotency, crash recovery, repeated revoke/reset, negative balances, separate byte/file accounts, credit capture at upload acceptance, resumed transfer debits without duplicate charging; shared-pool reservation races, low-space threshold changes, cache/temporary/staging budget isolation, lease expiration and crash cleanup; accurate effective-policy UI; bounded work and zero peer disclosure of private resource statistics.
